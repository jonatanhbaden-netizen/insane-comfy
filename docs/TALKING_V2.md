# Talking v2 — uploaded audio in, talking video out

**Primary:** `workflows/aiofm_talking_v2_upload.json` — your mp3/wav in,
lipsynced 9:16 video out. API copy: `aiofm_talking_v2_upload.api.json`.
**Optional:** `workflows/aiofm_talking_v2.json` — same graph, in-graph Chatterbox
TTS instead of a file.

```
python3 scripts/build_talking_v2.py --source upload --audio "yourfile.mp3"
python3 scripts/build_talking_v2.py --source tts
python3 scripts/build_talking_v2.py --fps 25 --out /tmp/v25.json     # experiment
```

## What it does

```
LoadAudio (your mp3)                     [--source tts: Chatterbox voice instead]
   ▼
NormalizeAudioLoudness -16 LUFS
   ▼  (optional phone EQ / compressor, both bypassed)
AudioConcat: 0.3 s lead-in silence       mouth starts closed, like the portrait
   ▼
AudioOrManualFrameCount(fps)          → F
   ▼
🛑 GUARD  F < 48   → abort               silent / failed audio source
🛑 GUARD  F > 477  → abort               audio past the cap was being CUT MID-WORD
   ▼
S = min(ceil((F+1)/8)*8, 480)
   ├─ S+1  → AudioFrameAdjuster          audio padded to EXACTLY S+1 frames
   └─ S/fps → subgraph duration          video renders round(S)+1 = S+1 frames
   ▼
ONE tensor feeds both the lipsync latent and the muxed track
```

Audio length == video length by construction. In upload mode Granite ASR
transcribes your audio so Gemma's performance description matches what is
actually said — without it Gemma invents a script and choreographs to the
invention (the original "motion is weird" bug).

## Failure modes engineered out

| Failure | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Silent/failed audio → video that "succeeds" | under-48-frame guard aborts |
| Audio over the cap cut mid-word, silently | over-477-frame guard aborts |
| Video one frame longer than its audio | audio targets S+1, duration targets S/fps |
| A/V drift over the clip | one tensor drives conditioning and mux |
| Off-grid frame counts | snap-up + `round()`; QA asserts frames ≡ 1 (mod 8) |
| Off-grid conditioning resample | deprecated `#294` cut out of the path |
| Non-deterministic motion | both seeds fixed; Gemma `sampling_mode: off` |
| TTS: engine dep missing → silent stub | `s3tokenizer` in `docker/Dockerfile` + the guard above |
| TTS: failed take replayed forever | audio cache off (the suite caches failures) |

## Measuring lipsync — and why no number here is a certification

`scripts/analyze_talking.py`. **Sign convention: positive `lag_frames` = the
mouth moves EARLIER than the sound.**

Two metrics were built and both were caught being wrong. This is the useful part
of the record:

1. **Aperture vs RMS envelope** — worthless. Its r=0.152 was the *median of its
   own permutation null*. Any conclusion from it was noise.
2. **d/dt inner-lip area vs d/dt envelope, gated on "peak prominence"** — the
   gate was a rubber stamp. Prominence subtracts the mean of a z-scored
   correlation curve, which is ~0 by construction, so it was just `r` renamed.
   Re-run against pure noise it declared results trustworthy **95% of the time**.
   Widening the search to ±20 lags also *raised* the noise floor (null median
   r≈0.22).

The current gate is an empirical **circular-shift permutation null**: the mouth
trace is shifted ≥1 s (destroying true alignment, preserving autocorrelation) and
the identical argmax is re-run 200×. A reading is only `trustworthy` when
`p_value < 0.05`. `r_null_p95` and `p_value` are reported so any number is
auditable.

`scripts/calibrate_sync.py` injects known A/V offsets and checks recovery
(measured slope 1.0). Note what that does and does not prove: it validates
**relative** comparisons (config A vs config B), **not** absolute frame accuracy.

**Real-video baseline** (`input/*.mp4`, 6 clips, measured 2026-08-03):

| Clip | r | lag |
|---|---|---|
| ref06 | 0.52 | −4 |
| ref04 | 0.41 | −3 |
| ref01 | 0.30 | −4 |
| 3× SnapInsta rips | 0.13–0.19 | −3, −7, −19 (noise) |

The three clean clips agree at **−3 to −4 frames with ~zero drift**; the noisy
rips (music, cuts, multiple speakers) scatter. Our renders measured r≈0.14–0.23 —
**below the noise floor**, so their lag readings are not significant. The one
defensible observation is that **real speech drives a much stronger mouth/audio
correlation (r 0.30–0.52) than our renders do (r 0.14–0.23)** — i.e. the
generated mouth is moving, but less tightly coupled to the audio than a real
face. Raising `r` toward the real-video band is a better optimisation target
than chasing a lag number.

Drift diagnosis needs ~8 s of speech; shorter clips report "clip too short"
rather than a bogus verdict.

## Open questions (require a pod)

1. **24 vs 25 fps.** LTX-2's audio VAE emits one token per 1/25 s. But
   `av_model.py::_prepare_positional_embeddings` converts video coords to
   absolute seconds via `* (1.0/frame_rate)`, so binding may be purely temporal
   and fps irrelevant. Two analyses disagree; the A/B (both graphs already built)
   was queued when the pod stopped. Run it first.
2. **Stage-1 sampler.** `euler_ancestral_cfg_pp` injects fresh noise each step.
   `euler` is an A/B candidate for temporal stability. Note CFG++ genuinely
   *needs* its uncond pass — it is not wasted compute, so this is a real
   trade, not a free win.
3. **Render-verify the shipped build** — the A/V-length fix and overrun guard
   have never been rendered.
